# Bar widget classes, style and colors

> **Files:** `qml/components/methods/BarLook.qml`, `qml/components/methods/BarWidgetStyle.qml`, `qml/components/methods/BarGradient.qml`, `qml/components/methods/BarAutoColors.qml`, `qml/components/bar/look/WidgetLook.qml`, `qml/components/bar/look/CellLook.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/base/BarIconWidget.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/base/BarWidget.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/base/AudioLevelWidget.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/base/BaseWidget.qml`, `qml/config/BarsConfig.qml`, `qml/config/BarStyle.qml`, `qml/config/schema/definitions/widgets/BarStyle.yaml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/Workspaces/WorkspaceCell.qml`
> **Config:** `Bars.style.*` (the shared `BarStyle`), per bar `style` (inheriting), widget color fields with `x-autoColor`
> **Related:** [Widget shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md) (the Shape, Kit and Placement layers), [Bar composition](composition.md), [Schema annotations](../core/schema-annotations.md) › Bar look

The base classes bar widgets extend, where their sizes come from, and how a bar's widget style is built: its five layers, the trait table where a style is defined, the colors (Auto colors, Split, Fill on hover, the bar gradient), which color fields show, the shared and per-bar style, and the Workspaces cells.

### Widget base classes
- **`bar/widgets/base/BarIconWidget.qml`** is the base for icon + label widgets.
  - It takes the `BarWidgetHost` inputs `barConfig`/`panel`/`screen`/`properties`, orientation and the configured colors.
  - Override `backgroundColor` for states. `hidden` collapses a widget with nothing to show.
  - It owns their pointer handling:
    - `clickable` + `acceptedButtons` → `clicked(button)`. The widget dims while pressed, and clicking is off while it is hidden.
    - `scrollable` → `scrolled(steps)`.
    - `hovered` is what a menu opened by a Button stays open for, and what a tooltip opens on. A widget taking no clicks or scrolls reads it from its hit area (or its own HoverHandler without one).
    - `dim` is for fades of the widget's own.
  - A widget's hover tooltip is a `BarToolTip` (`widget`, `text`, `shown`, `rich`): a `StyledToolTip` on the bar's inner side, built only while hovered.
- **`ValueStyleWidget`** (a `BarIconWidget`) draws a widget's value in its display style, and `ValueGraphic` is that graphic alone ([Display styles](display-styles.md)).
- **`AudioLevelWidget`** (a `ValueStyleWidget`) is shared by Volume and Microphone.
- **`bar/widgets/base/BarWidget.qml`** is the base for widgets that draw their own content. It has the same inputs plus `isVertical`.
- **`base/BaseWidget`** is the sizing wrapper under both.
- **`base/IconTextWidget`** (under `BarIconWidget`) lays the icon and label along the bar (an icon of several glyphs is one per line, stacked on a vertical bar and side by side `iconSpacing` apart on a horizontal one: the icon font's space is a whole icon wide), the label's letters centred on the icon's middle (`iconMiddle`) on a whole pixel rather than its box, which holds the descent and sat lowercase low (`TextBandPadding`: on the x-height with lowercase in it, a digit's height without, the font's bands so it moves only when lowercase comes or goes; upright under the icon, unpadded; turned along a vertical bar, it centres across it the same way); on a vertical bar the label is turned along it, or with `uprightLabel` small and upright under the icon (SystemStats' metrics, each one an IconTextWidget); a graphic of `leadLength` goes in its `lead` slot before the icon, or after it, where the label would be, with `leadAfterIcon` (a display style's ring, meters, ticker). Its label's digits are of equal width and it is measured with them as zeros, never under `minimumText` (a value's widest usual value, `"00%"`: only 100% widens it) and centred in that room, so a changing number never resizes the bar; the ticker and stacked's value (`ValueGraphic`) likewise. `textFormat` (Text.AutoText) is set to Text.PlainText by a widget showing text from outside (Next event's title), for the label, the ticker and both stacked lines. `iconScale` (Battery's and Tailscale's 1.1) draws the icon larger; the room at a run's end is kept at that height (`BarIconWidget.contentAcross`, [Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#backgrounds-and-shapes)).

### Sizes come from the bar
- `BarsConfig.enrichBarConfig` hands the shell's values to the pure `BarEnrich.enrich` (`env`, `BarsConfig.enrichEnv`; tests run the same path through `tests/barstyle/Bars.js`), which derives `extent` and where the widgets sit across (`crossStart`) from `thickness` and `edgePadding` (`BarEnds.crossGeometry`: solid and pill bars alike).
- **What's inside scales with the bar's thickness** from how it's set at 26 px (`BarsConfig.referenceThickness`): `fontSize` is `Appearance.baseFontSize × thickness/26 × textScale/100`, and `innerPadding`/`innerGap` are the bar's settings × thickness/26 (rounded). At 26 px and 100% a bar looks as it always has; at 16 or 40 px its text, icons and spacing are that much smaller or larger, and everything drawn from them (graphics, cells, the stacked style's two lines, which shrink to fit a text scale too large for the bar) follows.
- `radius` is the look's `widgetRadius`, else `Widget.radius`.
- These sizes stay flat on `barConfig` (`radius`, `fontSize`, `groupSpacing`, `joinGap`, `innerPadding`, `innerGap`, `splitRoom`): they're bar geometry, which non-style readers use too.

### The style system, in five layers
A bar's widget style (widgetStyle × shape × grouping × split × outline stroke × Fill on hover × the bar gradient, and Workspaces' cells) is built in layers, each with one job. Each layer reads the one before; none re-derives what another decides.

| Layer | Files | Owns | Never does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Look** | `methods/BarLook` | The bar's style resolved once (`barConfig.look`): inheritance, every "as drawn" rule, the trait table, the `$bar.*` context | Colors, geometry, drawing |
| **Paint** | `methods/BarWidgetStyle`, `methods/BarGradient` | Which color each part of a widget or cell takes, for a look and a state | Deciding what a style is, geometry |
| **Shape** | `methods/BarShapes`, `BarTabs`, `BarOutlines`, `BarCells` | Caps, segments, tabs, outline paths, hit tests, box rects | Colors, style names beyond the look's resolved fields |
| **Kit** | `qml/components/bar/look/` | The QML pieces that draw a box, line, split part, level and overlays from paint and shape (`WidgetLook` and `CellLook` hand a widget and a cell their paint) | Deciding anything a methods helper decides |
| **Placement** | `WidgetGroup`, `WidgetDecorations`, `BarWidgetHost`, `methods/BarRuns` | Where each background, mark and hit area sits | Re-deriving a style rule |

- **Nothing outside `BarLook` compares a style's name**. Paint, shape, kit and placement branch on the look's traits and resolved fields; a widget's schema asks `$bar.<trait>` ([Which color fields show](#which-color-fields-show)). `check_structure` fails a read of `widgetStyle` or `look.style` outside `BarLook` and the tests.
- The flow: `BarsConfig.enrichBarConfig` resolves `barConfig.look` (`BarLook.resolve`) and its colors (`barConfig.palette`, `BarWidgetStyle.palette`). A widget's `WidgetLook` (a cell's `CellLook`) turns what of the widget its look depends on into a `state` and asks paint for its `colors`. `WidgetDecorations` places a `WidgetBackground` under it, shaped by the shape helpers, which draws them with the kit's pieces.
- Shape, Kit and Placement are documented in [Widget shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md).
- **`tst_BarStyleMatrix`** (its expected data in `tests/barstyle/Matrix.js`) is the style system's regression net: every style × grouping × shape × ends × split option × Fill on hover × gradient × run × text color × cell state, through paint and shape, recorded. It passes unchanged unless an output is meant to change (its recording mode re-records it, and the diff is reviewed).

### Look: one resolved look per bar
- `BarLook.resolve(section, own, bar)` merges the shared style (`section`, `BarStyle.values`) with the bar's own (`own`, the bar's `style`: each key it leaves `null` the shared one's: `BarLook.merge`) and resolves it on the bar's geometry (`bar`: `thickness`, `innerPadding`, `innerGap`, `radius`, `far`; or null for the look alone). The look:
  - `values` (the merged BarStyle values, as saved), `style` (`widgetStyle`), `traits` (its record's, `traitsOf`: [The trait table](#the-trait-table))
  - `radius`, `textScale`, `tint` (0–1), `textColor` (a name, `""` for Auto), `hoverFill` (as drawn: on, for a style it applies to)
  - `outline` (`{ width, stroke }`, an outline's, else null), `line` (`{ width, side, farSide }`, underline's, else null)
  - `shape` (`{ name, ends, tabCorners, tabFillet, radius, outline, capped }`: the shape as drawn, what `BarShapes.segment` keeps content clear of; `tabFillet` is filled in by `enrichBarConfig`, `BarTabs.tabFillet`) and `grouping` (as drawn)
  - `split` (`{ icon, label, labelColor, cap, room, badgePad }`, split's, else null: `label` is the label's fill as drawn, a badge's none, and `cap` the divider's, `BarShapes.dividerCap`; the saved `splitLabelFill` and `splitDivider` are in `values`), `group` (`{ color, strokeColor }`: a merged run's), `gradient` (the gradient's color names, or null), `separators` (`{ style, color, thickness, gap }`), `accentLine` (`{ side, color, fade, width }`, or null)
- **As drawn:** an unboxed style draws rounded and separate; a powerline draws as separate where there's nothing to chain (a badge, tabs); a separate badge saved as a tab draws rounded; the separators draw none in a powerline, where widgets touch; plain text has no gradient.
- The shadow stays flat on `barConfig` (`shadow` / `shadowColor` / `shadowSize`, `BarLook.merge`): popouts, the border, blur backing and `WindowLookConfig` read it, and it isn't widget styling.
- `BarsConfig.lookOf(barConfig, section)` is the merge for the bar editor's forms (the values, with the bar's own fields); `BarsConfig.lookContext` is `BarLook.context` of the look alone ([Which color fields show](#which-color-fields-show)).

### The trait table
**A style is defined in one place: its record in `BarLook.table`.** Paint, shape and placement read its traits (`look.traits`; a cell's `BarLook.cellTraits`, a solid box's record or the look's), never its name, so a style is a record. Each field's meaning is commented in `BarLook`; a trait that depends on the look's other keys is a function of the look (`hasBox`, `opaque`, `gradientPart`, `autoContrast`), evaluated by `traitsOf`.

| Style | Box (`boxed`) | Paints (`box`: fill / stroke / line; text, icon) | Powerline (`opaque`) | In a merged run | Gradient (`gradientPart`) | Else |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `filled` | yes | its color; text and icon `own` (read on it) | opaque | no box, its color to its content (`groupCell`); the run's labels read on its fill | fill | Auto colors on the bar 1.3, merged on the run 3 |
| `tinted` | yes | its color at the tint; text and icon its color | see-through | `groupCell` | fill, at the tint | `hoverFills`, `tints` |
| `outline` | yes | stroke its color (fill clear, or its color cleared under Fill on hover); text and icon its color | see-through | `groupCell` | stroke | `hoverFills`, `stroke` |
| `split` | yes (a badge has none: `hasBox`) | none of its own: `iconPart`, an icon part filled in its color, its label apart (`split.label`) | opaque where the label has a color of its own | each icon part kept (`boxedInRun`); the run's gradient strip is a tint (`runTints`) | the icon part | `fallback: filled` (a widget that doesn't split, a cell) |
| `underline` | no | line its color; text and icon the bar's | – | – | the line | `line` |
| `accentText` (colored icons) | no | icon its color (kept under the bar's text color: `keepsIcon`); text the bar's | – | – | none | |
| `plain` | no | text and icon the bar's | – | – | none (no gradient at all) | `colored: false`: cells fade by state |
| `solid` (a cell's solid box, never a bar's) | yes | fill, stroke and line its color; content `own` | opaque | its box kept (`boxedInRun`) | fill | |

Every record has every field (`tst_BarLook`), and the table's keys are `BarStyle.widgetStyle`'s enum plus `solid`.

### Adding a widget style
1. **The enum value:** `widgetStyle`'s `enum` and `x-enumLabels` in `qml/config/schema/definitions/widgets/BarStyle.yaml`, and any settings of its own there, shown by sibling conditions on `widgetStyle`. `tst_BarLook` (`test_bar_style_conditions_agree_with_the_traits`) checks each style list in those conditions is one trait's styles: add the new style to the lists its traits put it in.
2. **One trait record** in `BarLook.table`, every field filled. New settings of its own become look fields in `resolve` (as `outline` and `line` are), null on other styles.
3. **A rule the traits can't state** becomes a new trait field, on every record, read where it's needed: never a comparison of the style's name (`check_structure`).
4. **Drawing code only if it draws something new:** a piece in `qml/components/bar/look/`, composed into `LookBox` or `WidgetBackground`. Its shapes come from the shape helpers, its colors from paint.
5. **Widgets' color fields** follow by themselves: they ask `$bar.<trait>` (`BarLook.contextTraits`), so a style that's boxed, underlined or fills its labels shows the right fields. A new question a widget needs is a new context trait.
6. **Tests and docs:** `tst_BarLook` rows for its traits, `tst_BarWidgetStyle` rows for what it paints, the shape tests for any new shape; `tst_BarStyleMatrix` passes unchanged (its recorded styles are fixed). The trait table above.

### Paint: colors over the look
- Widgets don't draw their own backgrounds. Each reports two things:
  - `hasBackground`: on by default for `BarIconWidget`, opt-in on `BarWidget`.
  - `accentColor`: its color for its current state, e.g. a critical battery or a muted mic. A widget names the color field it shows (`accentField`, `backgroundColor` by default on `BarIconWidget`, none on `BarWidget`), and `accentColor` is that field's color; one with a color of its own for a state (DevMode's and Updates' failure, NextEvent's soon, a custom widget's state) sets `accentField` to `""` then and its `accentColor` itself.
- Both bases work these out in one `bar/look/WidgetLook` (its `accentField`, `accentColor`, `autoColors`, `barOffset`/`barLength` set through the bases' aliases; `accentAuto`, `barSpot`, `colors` and `content` read back; `stateColor`, `hasState` and `appearance` feed colorText; `contentColor(color, part)` and `drawnColor(field)` are functions), so the gradient and Auto handling is written once.
- They draw in `colors` = `BarsConfig.widgetColors(barConfig, accent, foregroundName, widget)`, which gives `{ fill, stroke, indicator, text, icon, iconFill, gradient, textOnFill, textBack, iconBack, iconOnFill, level }`. `widget` is what of the widget itself its look depends on: `splits` (Split), `hovered` (Fill on hover), `spot` and `auto` (the bar gradient). `BarWidgetStyle.state` makes them the `state` paint reads (`{ gradient, splits, hovered, auto, run, group, solid }`; `group`: in a merged run on a merged bar), so `BarWidgetStyle.colors(look, palette, state, accent, own)` and `cell(look, palette, state, color, own, cellState)` take one shape: the bar's `look` and its colors resolved once (`barConfig.palette`: `barText`, `background`, `override`, `labelColor`, `group`, `groupStroke`, `gradientStops`, and `separator`, `accentLine` `{from, to}`, `hoverOutline`, `cellHover`: the bar's other colours, resolved once, a user's taken as set). `BarsConfig.widgetColors`, `cellColors`, `workspaceCellColors` and `groupColors` are thin wrappers over them.
  - Paint branches on the drawn style's traits alone: what a box paints each part in (`box`), an icon part and a label apart (`iconPart`), a run's gradient strip as a tint (`runTints`), a box kept in a merged run (`boxedInRun`), plain's colorless text (`colored`). A widget or cell that can't draw its style draws its `fallback`'s (split's filled). A widget's and a cell's boxes share one body (`_box`).
  - `widgetTextColor` (the look's `textColor`, the palette's `override`) overrides the automatic text color.
  - Text and icons on a fill the widget or run paints (filled, Fill on hover, split's icon part and its label on a color of its own, a merged filled run's labels, filled and solid cells, the bar gradient) read on it: `BarWidgetStyle.readableOn` keeps the color where its WCAG contrast is at least 4.5, else takes whichever of it, the theme's foreground and background, and black and white contrasts most (a clear fill is the bar: kept). Tinted, outline, underline, colored icons and plain text are the style's color on the bar, which Auto colors check already. `Utils.contrast` is the one WCAG formula, and `Utils.getContrastColor` picks black or white by it.
  - **Readable on its backdrop:** `textBack` and `iconBack` are the opaque colours the widget's text and icon sit on (a tint or translucent fill composited over the bar's background taken opaque; a pill's level is a second backdrop, so content reads on both). Content is nudged (`BarWidgetStyle.nudged`): a colour already reading on every backdrop is kept, else it's mixed toward white or black, whichever contrasts more, by the least amount that reaches the threshold (the hue kept; unreachable, the pole): 4.5 for anything that can be text, 3 for icons, graphics and badges. Only content is nudged, never fills, tints, strokes or lines, and Auto colour picking is unchanged. `widgetTextColor` (the palette override) skips the checks. `iconOnFill` says the icon sits on a fill (filled, hovered, split's icon part, a kept box in a merged run), where colorText leaves it alone; `level` is the pill's colour.
  - `textOnFill`: the widget's text sits on a fill it must read on (filled; a merged run on a filled or split bar, the run's fill counting, but for a split run under the gradient, which is its labels' tint; split's label on a color of its own; a widget filled on hover). "Colour the text by state" (`BarWidgetStyle.stateContent`) colours it only off a fill ([Display styles](display-styles.md)).
  - A tint (tinted, split's label, a tinted cell) and a level fade the color from its own opacity (`BarWidgetStyle.faded`), so a clear one (a widget with no color of its own: Workspaces' box with its background off) stays clear; `Qt.alpha` on it would draw black.
  - An outline is drawn in the bar's `outlineStroke` ([Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#outline-strokes)).
  - The styles' own options, and how they combine with the shapes, runs and the bar gradient: [Split](#split), [Fill on hover](#fill-on-hover), [Bar gradient](#bar-gradient), [Combinations](#combinations).
- Colors that are content rather than a background keep their colour and are read through `contentColor(color, "icon"|"text")` (nudged against what they sit on): badges, the DND icon (`iconOwnColor`), the VPN lock, Markets' trend colours, LockKeys' off keys (`drawnColor("inactiveColor")`). Workspace cells take `CellLook.under`, the widget's `textBack`.
- `backgroundLevel` (0–1, -1 for none) with `backgroundLevelColor`: a level its background fills to its own shape (`bar/look/LevelFill`; the custom widget's pill), in the background's caps with a rounded leading edge ([Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#background-states)). A graphic a widget draws itself reads in `colors.icon`, which contrasts with a filled background.

Core additions (`BarWidgetStyle`; the widgets move onto them in later slices): `state` also takes `muted` (the accent mixed `mutedMix` 0.45 toward the bar's background, content not on the widget's own fill through `muted(backs, color)`, floor 3, in place of opacity; a gradient it draws is cut to `1 - mutedMix` of its alpha, `mixColors` mixing it too), `pressed` (content mixed `pressMix` toward its backdrop, then nudged back), and for cells `pulse` (0-1 toward its muted self, `mixColors`) and `hover`. `colors()` also gives `headText` (the icon part's text, 4.5), `mark`, `graphic { line, track, area, caption }` (opaque), and `textOwnFill`/`iconOwnFill`. `content(colors, color, part)` reads a colour of the widget's own on `text`, `icon`, `mark` or `headText`; `stateContent` also returns `graphic`; `fieldColor(palette, spot, color, givesWay, role)` is a field's colour on the fade (under the bar's text colour a neutral or active one takes it); `badge(palette, colors, color)` gives `{ fill, text }`. A pill's level is the icon's colour at 35% where white or black can read on both backdrops, else a white or black sheen, else weaker. Empty cells mix their content `emptyMix` 0.6 toward their backdrop (floor 3). `tests/tst_BarWidgetStyle.qml` sweeps every look, grouping, hover, gradient, split, level, muted and colorText on a dark and a light theme, and cells likewise.

### Split
- `widgetStyle: split` sets a widget's icon part apart from its label (the `iconPart` trait). The icon part (the icon, and a lead graphic before it: a display style's ring, fill, segments or sparkline; ticker and stacked are the label side) is filled with the widget's color (`iconFill`), its icon in `own`.
- `splitIcon` joined: one box round the whole widget, cut at the icon part's end by `splitDivider` ([Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#split)). The label's part (`splitLabelFill`) is a tint of the color (`tintOpacity`), a color of its own (`splitLabelColor`, its text reading on it) or none (the bar's); its text is the widget's color on a tint or none. `widgetTextColor` overrides both texts.
- `splitIcon` badge: no box; a small shape behind the icon part, the label on the bar in the widget's color (the look's `split.label` is none). A badge has no box to chain or hang (`hasBox`): its powerline draws as separate and a separate badge isn't offered the tab shape (one saved with it draws rounded: `BarLook.resolve`).
- Only a widget showing an icon part and a label splits (an after-icon graphic, ticker or stacked, counts as the label: `BarShapes.iconTextSplit`; one with no icon doesn't split). An `IconTextWidget` does with an icon (a glyph, or an image in its place: `iconSource`, Window's app icon) or a lead graphic, and a label (`IconTextWidget.splits`, which `BarIconWidget` hands `widgetColors` in `widget`). A `BarWidget` drawing its own content does with a head (`SplitLead`: an icon, a short text or an item of its own, at its start, its content after it): Time (`lead`: an icon, or the date moved before the time), the tray (`leadIcon`) and Workspaces (`lead`: an icon, or the active workspace's label as its cell shows it; only in its box, none when its cells are the run's segments). Both work out where the gap and the icon part are with `BarShapes.splitPart`, and the widget reports `splits`, `splitAt` and `splitPart`, which `BarWidgetHost.split` reads. A widget with no text color field (the tray, Workspaces) reads its head in the field's default (`WidgetLook`).
- The labels that let a widget split are each its own switch, off by default so a bar that doesn't split looks as before: Volume's and Microphone's `showDevice` (the device's name, cut at 24 characters, before the percentage), Notifications' `label` (the count, which then hides the badge, or the newest alert's app), Window's `appIcon`.
- Icon-only and text-only widgets, a `BarWidget` with no head (Media, a head left at none, …: `widgetColors` called without `splits`) draw as filled (split's `fallback` trait), and so do Workspaces' cells. In a merged run such a widget fills its own box (in the caps it has alone, as a level), so the run reads as a row of tabs like the split widgets' icon parts, rather than showing only a colored icon or colored text on the run.
- **Merged:** each widget keeps its own icon part on the run's `groupColor`, its label reading on the run (`splitLabelFill` is hidden). **Powerline:** each segment has its own icon part.
- A split bar's widgets keep `splitRoom` between the two parts instead of `spacing` (the look's `split.room`, `BarShapes.splitRoom`: joined, the padding either side of the divider and its depth; a badge, its reach `badgePad` plus the spacing).

### Boxes: one per item
- A widget showing several items (SystemStats' metrics, Lock keys' keys, Privacy's indicators, Markets' symbols, ClaudeUsage's accounts) can draw one box per item: its `boxes` setting (One, or One per item), which the `boxes: {variant: each}` part of its `schema.yaml` adds, with the items on the setting (`x-boxItems`: [schema annotations](../core/schema-annotations.md)).
- SystemStats with One draws its metrics in one box, each its own icon part on a split bar (`innerSplits`, [Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#split)); the grid keeps `splitRoom` between them.
- With One per item, `BarContainer.widgetModel` expands its entry into one per item (`methods/BarBoxes.expand`, the items from `BarsConfig.boxItems`): the same properties, the item as `box` (a Markets symbol's or ClaudeUsage account's id, `MarketMath.entries` the widget's own list too), its `configIndex` the entry's. Each is hosted by a `BarWidgetHost` of its own, so each box takes everything a widget does with no drawing code of its own: split, its own pill level, its state color, Fill on hover, its place in a powerline chain or merged run, the gradient. One item draws whole, as with One.
- The items come from config only (the options switched on, Markets' watchlist, ClaudeUsage's accounts), never live values: the section's Repeater models them. A box whose item the machine can't report (SystemStats' GPU on a machine with none) hides itself (no size, as a hidden widget).
- The host hands the module its `box` (`BarWidget.box`, `BarIconWidget.box`), and the widget draws just that item: SystemStats one metric (its segment's split passed on as the widget's), Lock keys and Privacy their key's icon with a short name (so they split), Markets one symbol (or the portfolio) without cycling, ClaudeUsage one account by its name.
- To the bar editor it stays one widget: one chip, one form, one drag; every box shares its `configIndex`, so its selection and hover outline every box, and each box opens the widget's popout from where it is.
- The boxes are the bar's widget spacing apart, as separate widgets are.

### Fill on hover
- `hoverFill` (shown for tinted and outline: the `hoverFills` trait, the boxed styles that aren't filled already; not split, which is filled where it shows its color) fills a widget under the pointer with its color, its text and icon turning to its own text color (`own`), as if filled.
- The decision is the look's `hoverFill`; `colors()` reads `state.hovered` (`BarsConfig.widgetColors`' `widget` holds it), and the widget passes its hit area's `hovered` (`BarIconWidget`, `BarWidget`), so the background, text and icon all follow one output. With the option on, an unfilled outline's fill is its color cleared, so the fill fades in (`ColorGlide`, `animFast`) from nothing rather than through black.
- In a merged run only the hovered widget's own part fills, in the caps it has alone (`levelStartCap` / `levelEndCap`, as a level), its outline left to the run; a press tints that part on top.
- A `BarWidget` opts out with `fillsOnHover: false`: Workspaces, whose cells fill instead.

### Auto colors
- A widget color field with an `x-autoColor` role is Auto when empty, which is its default.
- `BarsConfig.enrichBarConfig` fills Auto fields in the widgets it hands out (`BarsConfig.withAutoColors(widgets, look)`; the fields per type are in `BarsConfig.autoColorFields`, read from the schema by `BarEnrich.autoColorFields`). Widgets just resolve `properties.X`.
- The pure `methods/BarAutoColors` picks colors along the bar's shown widgets, every section in order. The roles are:
  - **`accent`:** the widget's own color, a base08–0F accent unlike its neighbours'. Fixed colors count as neighbours too. Every accent is used before any repeats, and an accent is kept apart from the widget's own warning/critical colors.
  - **`alt`:** a second state, as unlike its accent as possible.
  - **`warning`/`critical`:** the theme's `warning`/`error`.
  - **`rest`:** the same pick as `off`, for a widget's usual idle state (LockKeys' inactive, IdleInhibitor's inactive, Drives', Pomodoro's and Stopwatch's idle); unlike `off` it gives way to the bar gradient. Muted, disconnected, paused, warning and critical keep their own colours so they stand out.
  - **`off` and `neutral`:** the subtlest of base01–04 that still stands out. They are for an off state, or for a box whose content has its own colors (the tray's and Workspaces').
- Each color must stand out (WCAG contrast) from what it's seen on: the bar, or a filled or split merged run's `groupColor` (the `autoContrast` trait: `{ onRun, minContrast }`). A tinted or outlined run's widgets are seen on the bar (through the tint, or inside the run's line).
  - A fill only has to show (contrast 1.3).
  - Strokes and icons have to read (contrast 3); drawn text is nudged to 4.5 on its backdrop (above). Split's color is its icon part's fill, and also its label's text unless the label has a color of its own or sits on a merged run.
- Text colors are not Auto. Workspaces' cells are not Auto either, except the active one.

### Bar gradient
- `gradient` (`gradientFrom`, `gradientVia` (optional, halfway), `gradientTo`; group "Colors", hidden under Plain text) runs one gradient along the whole bar, left to right (top to bottom on a vertical bar). The look gives it as `gradient` (the color names, start to end, or null: off, or plain), and the palette as `gradientStops`.
- It's measured against the bar's full length (`BarContainer.length`), not its first to last widget, so its colors don't shift when widgets come and go. Places are the layout's (`Section.mainPos`, `allocation.offsets`), never the gliding ones, so nothing re-reads them each frame of a glide: `WidgetGroup.barStart`/`barLength` → each host's `barStart`/`barLength` → its module's `barOffset`/`barLength`, from which `WidgetLook` works out `barSpot`, where its middle sits (0–1: `BarGradient.spot`, the formula a run's middle and `BarWidget.barSpotOf` use too).
- **Only an Auto own color gives way to it.** `BarsConfig.withAutoColors` hands each widget its Auto fields' roles (`autoColors`, `{ key: role }`), and `accentAuto` is whether its `accentField` is Auto with a role that gives way (`WidgetLook._gradedRoles`: `accent` and `rest`); `drawnColor(field)` gives any field's colour as drawn the same way. A color set on the widget, and its state colors (the other roles: alt, off, warning, critical; a recording, a low battery), keep theirs. One widget type has at most one `accent` field (`tst_Config`).
- `BarWidgetStyle.colors` then draws the widget in the gradient's color at `barSpot` (`BarGradient.gradientAt`; its text readable on it), and says in `gradient` what of its background shows the gradient itself (the drawn style's `gradientPart`: the fill when filled, the tint when tinted, the stroke when outlined (every `outlineStroke`: `OutlineStroke` takes the slice too), the line when underlined, split's icon part (`iconFill`: the joined part's shape or the badge, each its own slice of the widget's); null under colored icons, whose icon takes the color where it sits). A widget filled on hover fills with its slice in full. Its background draws its slice of it there (`BarGradient.gradientSlice`: three stops from where it starts to where it ends along the bar, `gradientStart`/`gradientEnd`), in a path shape (`ShapeSlice`: a `Rectangle`'s border takes no gradient) or, for the line and a badge, a `Rectangle`'s gradient (`RectSlice`).
- **A merged run** is one gradient strip in place of `groupColor` (hidden while the gradient's on): its run background takes the slice from the run's start to its end (`BarsConfig.groupColors` with the run's middle, `BarWidgetStyle.runColors`). Its widgets' labels read on the strip where they sit, and an Auto icon too (through a tint or outline it takes the gradient's color there); a set or state color stays on its icon. A split run's strip is its labels' tint (`tintOpacity`, the `runTints` trait), under the widgets' icon parts (and the boxes of those that don't split), each its own slice in full, so the row of tabs still reads; its labels are in the widget's color, as on split's own tint.
- **Workspaces**: the active cell takes the gradient's color where its middle sits, its `activeColor` Auto (`WorkspaceCellRow._activeLook`, through `BarWidget.barSpotOf`, which maps a point in the widget to the bar). The box (Auto `neutral`) and the other cells keep theirs, and so does the active cell in a merged run, which is a strip of the gradient itself (the cell would vanish into it).
- With no place on a bar (the bar editor's chips and the custom widget preview) a widget shows its Auto color.

### Combinations
How the four widget-style features meet (each decided in `BarLook`, `BarWidgetStyle` or the shape helpers, with test rows):
- **Split + tab:** both parts reach the edge the tab hangs from; the divider keeps its shape (`BarTabs.sides`). A badge has no box: a separate badge isn't offered tab.
- **Split + gradient:** the icon part (or badge) takes its slice; in a merged run see Bar gradient above. **Split + powerline:** each segment its own icon part (a badge draws as separate).
- **Split + Fill on hover:** not offered (split is already filled where it shows its color).
- **Tab + outline strokes:** every stroke is open along the edge (dashed, dotted, double, ends only: [Shapes and runs](widget-shapes-runs.md#outline-strokes)). **Tab + Fill on hover:** the hovered tab fills in its own shape, out to the edge. **Tab + gradient:** each tab its slice, fillets included. Tabs never chain as a powerline.
- **Gradient + outline strokes:** the stroke takes the slice in every stroke. **Gradient + Fill on hover:** the hovered widget fills with its slice.
- **Merged + outline:** the run is outlined in `groupStrokeColor` ("Merged outline", default accent; shown for outline + merged, and `groupColor` hidden there, as an outline run has no background), or with the gradient on, one strip of it. Its widgets' colors move to their icons, as on any merged run, and Workspaces' cells draw no borders of their own (see Workspaces cells).
- **Merged + Ends only:** a mark at the run's two ends only.

### Which color fields show
- A widget's color fields show in the bar editor's inspector only in the bar looks where they draw something.
  - Their `x-showIf` reads the look of the selected bar through `$bar.<key>` keys (`BarsConfig.lookContext`: `BarLook.context`): a `BarStyle` value, with shape and grouping as drawn, or a trait the user sees (`BarLook.contextTraits`, each a function of the look): `boxed`, `merged`, `underlined`, `ownColorShows`, `textColorShows`, `cellsFilled` ([Schema annotations](../core/schema-annotations.md) › Bar look). A widget's schema never lists styles: what a style shows is its traits'.
  - `tst_SchemaLayout` (`test_real_schema_showIf_targets_exist`) checks that each such key names a `BarStyle` field or a trait, never `widgetStyle`, in a bar widget's options only; `tst_BarLook` holds each trait per style.
- **The widget's own color** (`backgroundColor` titled "Color", and its state colors: the `WidgetColor` definition). It is the fill when filled, the tint or outline when tinted or outlined, the icon part's fill (and the label's tint or text) when split, the line when underlined, and the icon when merged or under colored icons.
  - Hidden under Plain text (`$bar.ownColorShows`).
  - A widget with no icon (Time, the tray's box) shows it only when boxed and not merged, or when underlined (`$bar.boxed` and not `$bar.merged`, or `$bar.underlined`).
  - Notifications' DND and badge colors are content, so they always show.
- **`foregroundColor`.** It is read only by a filled widget that isn't merged, a split one (its icon on the icon part, merged or not), or a tinted or outlined one with Fill on hover (its text while filled), and only while the bar's `widgetTextColor` is Auto (`$bar.textColorShows`, every widget's alike). Elsewhere the text is the bar's.
- **Workspaces** (its popouts draw cells in the same style):
  - `showBackground` shows only for boxed styles (`$bar.boxed`, split among them). `backgroundColor` also needs the background on and the grouping not merged.
  - `activeColor` and `occupiedColor` hide under Plain. `emptyColor` (and the None labels choice) shows only when boxed or underlined. `urgentColor` always shows: under Plain an urgent cell's content takes it. With `solidBoxes` on, the active, occupied and empty colors show under every style.
  - `textColor` shows only while the bar's text color is Auto, when cells are filled boxes or filled while hovered (`$bar.cellsFilled`: filled, split's cells, and a style Fill on hover applies to with it on, as `BarWidgetStyle.cell` draws a hovered cell's label in it) and show numbers, dots, labels of one's own, names or the grid's arrow, or with `solidBoxes` on.
  - Cells follow the widget shape: pills under capsule (`BarsConfig.cellRadius`), otherwise the radius less the inset. With the background off they're segments of the bar's run in its shape ([Shapes and runs › Parts as segments](widget-shapes-runs.md#parts-as-segments)).
- The bar's own look fields read the look it uses too (`BarsPanel` hands its form `BarsConfig.lookOf(bar)`). Their own conditions are on sibling keys (the settings page has no `$bar` context), and `tst_BarLook` checks each style list there is one trait's styles. The separator fields hide in a boxed powerline, where the look's separators are none.

### Shared and per-bar style (`BarStyle`)
- A bar's widget style, accents and shadow are a `BarStyle`: the shared one is `Bars.style` (shown in Look & Feel, reader `BarStyle`), and each bar has its own `style` (`x-inherit: Bars.style`) whose keys it leaves `null` take the shared one's.
- `BarLook.merge` takes both, its shadow as flat `shadow` / `shadowColor` / `shadowSize`; `BarLook.resolve` makes the look of them, which `enrichBarConfig` sets as `barConfig.look`. `widgetRadius` stays `null` where both leave it (`Widget.radius` then); `textScale` always has a value.
- The flat keys stay as saved, so no migration: per-key inheritance (`null` = the shared style's), the bar editor's per-section "This bar's own" switches and the `x-showIf` sibling conditions are built on them. The nested shape is the runtime look's.
- The bar editor's Style tab shows the style in the [Bar style block](style-block.md), one section per `x-group` (`BarsConfig.styleGroups`, from `SchemaLayout.objectGroups`), each with a "This bar's own" switch: `BarManager.ownsStyle` / `setStyleOwned` (copies what it inherits, or sets the section back to `null`) / `updateStyleField`.

### Workspaces cells
- Workspaces sits on a background like the rest (`backgroundColor`). Its box is Auto (`neutral`).
- Its cells (`workspaces/WorkspaceCell`) are inset by `innerGap` inside that box, `spacing` apart (none, `innerGap` or twice it: `BarsConfig.workspaceCellSpacing`).
- A hovered cell's box is `hoverColor` (`BarsConfig.cellHoverColor`; Auto is `Theme.backgroundHighlight`). On the bar, a cell reads hover from the widget's hit area (`hitArea.hovers(cell)`), which lies over the cells: their own `MouseArea` misses the leave after a click and stays lit. In a popout it uses its own.
- **One cell look** (`bar/look/CellLook`, its rules `BarCells.cellLook` and `cellLength`): worked out once per switcher (`Workspaces` makes it, from the bar's look, the widget's options, whether it's on the bar and its place in the bar's run) or popout (`WorkspacePopoutPanel`), and handed to its rows, strips and cells: whether they're in a merged run, boxed, framed by a box or a line, their size, corners, spacing and gaps, their shapes and room as segments (`shapeOf`, `roomOf`) and their colors (`colorsOf`, `workspaceColorsOf`).
- Cells draw in the same style through `BarsConfig.cellColors` → `BarWidgetStyle.cell`, which gives `{ fill, stroke, indicator, content, showsHover, gradient }` from the cell's state color; `cellColors`' last argument is an options object (`{ hovered, spot, solid, onBar }`).
  - **Filled, tinted or outlined** styles draw boxes (`bar/look/LookBox`, the cell's and the sliding active box's, shaped by `BarCells.cellBox`), an outline in the bar's `outlineStroke`, with the cell's label (`labels`: its number, a small dot, or nothing) inside; split draws them filled.
  - **Fill on hover:** a lit cell (`lit`, handed to `cellColors` as `{ hovered }`) fills with its state color, its content in its own text color, and `showsHover` tells the cell to draw no `hoverColor` box of its own. The sliding active box takes the hover while the active cell is lit. Solid boxes are filled already: they keep the `hoverColor` box.
  - **Styles with no box** (underline, colored icons, plain) draw the cell's number or a dot straight on the bar, or for `labels: none` only underline's line (colored icons and plain, with no line, draw a dot; the option is hidden there). The dot becomes a pill when wide, with the cells narrowed to fit (for `none` too).
  - `showAppIcons` puts app icons in place of the label on occupied cells, under every compositor, laid out by `appIconsLayout`: `largest` the biggest window's alone; `row` up to `appIconsMax` icons side by side along the bar, the cell growing to fit (`WorkspaceCells.rowLength`), packed in its middle; `square` up to four in a block the size of a normal cell, which never changes size (one full size, two half size side by side, three as two over one, four a 2×2: `WorkspaceCells.squareSlots`, filled top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right). Row and Square choose by size and order by place (`WorkspaceCells.chosen`): the biggest windows up to the limit, each app once by its biggest window (`CompositorManager.windowsBySize` + `WorkspaceCells.distinctApps`), or with `appIconsCount: windows` every window counting (three terminals, three icons), then in the order they sit on screen: on a strip monitor strip order (`CompositorManager.stripWindows`, vertical strips too), elsewhere left to right by x, then top to bottom (`WorkspaceCells.byPosition`, floating windows by their own place); an app sits at its biggest window's place. No "+n" marker. On a vertical bar the row runs along the bar; the square is the same block. A label of one's own (`workspaceLabels`) wins over it, and it over a compositor name (`useNames`): [Workspaces](../hyprland/workspaces.md) › The bar widget.
    - For underline, each cell is underlined itself and the widget's own line is off.
    - An empty cell's content is the bar text, faded.
    - Plain tells the states apart by fading alone, but for urgent, whose content takes `urgentColor`.
  - **In a merged run** (`onBar`, the bar's callers' `hitArea !== null`, on a merged bar: the state's `group`), the run draws the background, as for a widget: filled, tinted and outlined cells draw no box or border (the `groupCell` trait), their state color moving to their content (an empty one's faded, reading on a filled run's color or, through a tint or outline, the bar). Solid boxes stay filled boxes, and split's cells fill theirs (as a widget that doesn't split fills its own box in a split run). Fill on hover still fills the hovered cell, with no border. A popout's cells keep their boxes: it isn't in a run. Such a cell counts as unboxed (`CellLook.boxed`: its dot full size, an active label bold), but for a solid or split one (the `boxedInRun` trait).
  - **As segments** (the Background off): the sliding active box takes the active cell's shape, fitted against the cell before, and an Auto active color draws its slice of the bar's gradient from where the box starts to its end (`cell`'s `gradient`, the part of the box that takes it, as a widget's; `WorkspaceCellRow._activeSlice`).
  - **Solid boxes** (`solidBoxes`): every cell (active, occupied, urgent, empty) is a box filled with its state's color on any bar style, its outline and line (where the bar's style draws them) in that color too (`BarsConfig.cellColors(…, { solid })` → `BarWidgetStyle.cell` with the state's `solid`: the `solid` record's traits, `BarLook.cellTraits`; an Auto active color still takes the bar gradient, and an outline bar's `outlineStroke` draws in the fill's color). Its content takes the color that reads on the fill, as filled's (`textColor` for active, occupied and urgent; the theme's foreground for empty), or the bar's text color where set. The cells count as boxed (`CellLook.boxed`): square on every style, dots smaller, and the active box (`CellLook.slides`) slides on every style. Labels' None is offered with it on any style.
  - **Urgent** (the workspace shape's `urgent`): `BarsConfig.workspaceCellColors` gives it the `"urgent"` state in `urgentColor`, which beats occupied but not active; it colours the box, line or content as the other states do. With `urgentPulse` its box or line (its content where it has neither) fades in and out, every urgent cell of a row or popout in step (`workspaces/UrgentPulse`, one looping animation, running only while one is urgent and `Appearance.animations`). Where a cell has neither box nor line (plain, colored icons) and shows an app icon, a small dot in `urgentColor` at its corner shows it.
