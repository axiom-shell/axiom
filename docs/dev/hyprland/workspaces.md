# Workspaces (layout, navigation, the bar widget)

> **Files:** `qml/config/WorkspacesConfig.qml`, `qml/services/compositor/CompositorManager.qml`, `qml/services/compositor/WorkspaceRemap.qml`, `qml/services/compositor/HyprlandManager.qml`, `qml/components/methods/WorkspaceGeometry.qml`, `qml/components/methods/WorkspaceCells.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/Workspaces/Workspaces.qml`, `qml/components/bar/widgets/Workspaces/`, `qml/components/surfaces/workspaces/`, `qml/components/views/Settings/WorkspacesStatus.qml`
> **Config:** `Workspaces.*` (`layout`, `count`, `columns`, `rows`, …)
> **Related:** [Workspace strips](workspace-strips.md), [Keybinds](keybinds.md), [Bar composition](../bar/composition.md), [Shared UI pieces](../core/shared-ui-pieces.md)

One workspace layout serves everything that shows workspaces or moves between them:
- the `Workspaces` bar widget, with its `WorkspaceGrid` and `WorkspaceStrips` popouts
- the workspace overview (`surfaces/workspaces/`; where the compositor has its own, `supports.overview`, `WorkspaceOverlay.compositorOverview` opens that instead: [contract](../compositor/contract.md) › Capabilities)
- the `WorkspacesMap` module
- the launcher's `/ws`
- the `workspaces` IPC target.

### Layouts
- `standard`: ids 1..`count`, shared by every monitor.
- `perMonitor`: each monitor gets its own block of `count` ids.
- `grid`: each monitor gets a block of `columns × rows` ids.
- `scrolling`: ids as `perMonitor` (`count` each), and every monitor a horizontal strip (`WorkspacesConfig.strips` is `*` whatever the list holds; [workspace strips](workspace-strips.md)). Offered only with `supports.strips` and not `stripsAlways` (`x-enumShowIf`). On Hyprland its workspaces are dynamic, as niri's, kept by axiom ([workspace strips](workspace-strips.md) › The scrolling layout). A config carrying it elsewhere (sway) works as `perMonitor`: the strips are ignored there (`CompositorManager.stripDirection`), and nothing else tells the two apart.
- Blocks are numbered over every monitor the saved monitor profiles name (`MonitorsConfig.profiles`, by their rules' `output`), connected or not, then the connected ones no profile names, each part in a stable order (`WorkspaceGeometry.orderMonitors`: the primary first, then by output identity, ignoring case and never the locale). So a hotplug never moves a named monitor's block: an unplugged one keeps its block for when it comes back (a monitor no profile names can move when another such one connects or leaves before it in that order; its windows follow it: [Hotplugs](#hotplugs-an-unplugged-monitors-windows)). A monitor whose description isn't known yet (Hyprland's, until the monitors are first refreshed) matches a `desc:` rule by the connector the rule last saw it on (its `label`), after any rule naming that connector itself. `WorkspaceGeometry.blockBases` gives each output's first id; `CompositorManager`, `HyprlandManager` and `NiriManager` all pass it the profiles, so their bases agree, and the remap places blocks by the same bases (`_bases`).
- Where the compositor's workspaces belong to an output (`supports.outputWorkspaces`, niri), every layout gets blocks, `standard` too (`CompositorManager.perMonitorBlocks`), and nothing is remapped.
- **Dynamic workspaces** (`supports.dynamicWorkspaces`, niri): the compositor makes and removes them itself, one column per output, so no layout applies. The section's fields are greyed out under its `x-intro` (`WorkspacesStatus`), except `wrap`. Blocks are the backend's `blockSize` long (`CompositorManager.blockSize`), `workspaceIds` are the ones it has, `workspaceGrid` is false whatever the layout says (so the Workspaces widget's `showActiveIcon` is hidden), the widget's `showAppIcons` works as anywhere else, `relativeNumbers` shows whatever the layout, and the overview is one column.
- Nothing else holds a workspace count or grid size.

### Ids
- `CompositorManager.workspaceBase(output)` / `workspaceIds(output)` give an output's ids (by its name).
- The overview lays them out `boardColumns × boardRows`. In the standard layout it shows windows from every monitor, each placed against its own monitor's origin.

### Navigation
- Everything goes through `CompositorManager`, so every switch slides the same way, on every compositor:
  - `goToWorkspace(id, mode, output)`
  - `stepWorkspace(direction, mode)`
  - `nthWorkspace(n, mode)`
  - `focusWindow`, for focusing a window on another workspace (used by the overview, dock, launcher and notifications).
- Never dispatch `hl.dsp.focus({ workspace })` or `{ window }` directly. (Rule: AGENTS.md › Services.)
- `CompositorManager` works out the legs (`{ id, axis }`) and hands them to the backend's `travel`; a backend without `workspaceSlide` just goes to the last one ([contract](../compositor/contract.md)).
- `mode` is `go`, `move` or `moveSilent`.
- Surfaces on a monitor pass their output's name; the focused one is the default. In a grid, an id outside that monitor's block is refused.
- **Dynamic workspaces**: up and down go through the output's list (`wrap` wraps round it, `workspaceWrapped` along the row), left and right to the output that way (`WorkspaceGeometry.outputToward`; the backend's `focusOutput`, or `moveWindowToOutput` for `move`/`moveSilent`), nothing without one. `nthWorkspace(n)` is the n-th of the list.
- **In a grid** a move only reaches the monitor's own ids.
  - It goes sideways first, then vertically, sliding along each axis in turn (`animate`). Only a switch between two ids of the block slides in legs: one past it (the standard layout's past `count`, `/ws 15`) is a plain switch.
  - Each leg of a two-leg trip takes `_legSpeed` of the configured time.
- **The slide** (`HyprlandManager.travel`) briefly overrides Hyprland's `workspaces` animation, then puts it back. The animation is read with the options (`HyprlandManager._workspaceAnim`, null with `animations:enabled` off: no slide, just the switch) at startup and on config reload; each leg keeps the one its travel read, so a read between two legs can't stop the second.
- Keybinds call `axiom ipc workspaces …` rather than scripts of their own.

### Layout changes (remap)
- The compositor's workspaces stay where they are when the layout or size changes, so `CompositorManager` remaps them, through its `_remap` (`compositor/WorkspaceRemap`, a part it holds).
  - `WorkspaceRemap.remap` runs 200 ms after `perMonitorBlocks`/`size`/`workspaceGrid` change, or the block order moves an output's block with the same outputs connected (`_bases`: a saved profile naming a new monitor, a new primary), starting from the layout it last applied (its `bases` too). A hotplug doesn't remap the layout: it records the new bases, and re-homes what it left outside its output's block (below).
- `WorkspaceGeometry.remapWorkspaces` (pure, tested) sends each workspace's windows to the same place in its own monitor's new ids.
  - The first monitor in monitor order keeps a contested id; the rest take free ones, merging only when none is free.
  - It picks each monitor's new active workspace. When the change turns the grid on (`workspaceGrid` true where the state file below keeps it false, so the config settling at the shell's start never counts), every monitor goes to its block's centre instead (`WorkspaceGeometry.centreFocus`).
- The backend carries the plan out (`applyRemap`). On Hyprland one Lua chunk does it, with the `workspaces` animation off:
  1. windows go to `name:axiom-remap-<id>`, created on their own monitor, so a chain like 10 → 11, 11 → 12 can't merge
  2. every monitor goes to `name:axiom-remap-mon-<i>`, so its old (maybe empty) workspace is gone before its id is reused
  3. windows go to their ids
  4. monitors go to theirs, the focused one last.

  A monitor step runs only while Hyprland has that monitor on (`hl.get_monitor`, nil for a disabled one): a plan made as a profile turns one off, or one is unplugged, skips it rather than fail (`hl.focus.monitor: monitor not found`) and send its workspace to whichever monitor has the focus.
- IPC `workspaces reconcile` (`WorkspaceRemap.reconcile`) runs the remap for the current layout, re-homing workspaces left outside their monitor's ids.

### Hotplugs (an unplugged monitor's windows)
- **What the compositors do** (checked 2026-11-07 on nested sessions): Hyprland 0.57 (`hyprctl output create headless` / `output remove`) moves an unplugged monitor's workspaces, a shown special workspace and a workspace under axiom's `m[<name>]` strip rule included, with their windows, to its first remaining monitor (not the focused one), and moves them back when a monitor of that name connects again. With every monitor gone it keeps them on its headless `FALLBACK`. sway 1.12 (headless, `create_output` / `output <name> unplug`) moves them to the remaining output; the scratchpad isn't an output's. niri moves an output's workspaces to another output and back when it returns (its own documentation; not checked here: a nested niri has one output it can't drop). axiom writes no workspace-to-monitor rule, so nothing it writes holds a workspace on a monitor that's gone, and no window is left on no output.
- **A monitor that stops hosting workspaces** counts as unplugged: unplugged, turned off (a profile closing the lid, a Monitors trial) or made a mirror. Hyprland moves its workspaces as for an unplug (checked on a nested Hyprland 0.57: `hl.monitor({ output = "HQ", disabled = true })` and `mirror = "WAYLAND-1"`), and its monitor list (`hyprctl monitors`) leaves it out. A mirror starting or stopping sends no monitor event, only its `wl_output` goes or comes, so `HyprlandManager` reads the monitors again whenever `Quickshell.screens` changes; without that `outputs` kept a mirror listed, nothing was re-homed, and Hyprland didn't move its workspaces back when the mirror stopped. sway lists a disabled output as inactive, which `SwayManager.outputs` leaves out.
- **What axiom does.** With blocks (perMonitor, grid), the workspaces the compositor moved keep their ids, outside the receiving monitor's block, so its bar, overview, steps and number keys never show them (only the window switcher and the dock would). So 3 s after a hotplug (`WorkspaceRemap._hotplugged`, `_rehome`; the grace period: a dock, an MST hub or DPMS dropping an output for a moment as the machine suspends or resumes has it back by then, and the compositor has put its workspaces back, so nothing moves), each workspace an unplugged monitor's block held, or its own monitor's block before the hotplug moved it (an unnamed monitor's, above), and now outside its monitor's block, moves whole into the first workspace of that block with no window (`WorkspaceGeometry.rehomeWorkspaces`, pure and tested), lowest id first, so the layout reads the same: a workspace with windows is never merged into, unless the block has no empty one left, when the rest join its last. Through the backend's `applyRemap`. Every other window stays (one put on another monitor's ids on purpose too), and each monitor keeps the workspace it shows unless that one moved. The debug log says `re-homing workspaces after a hotplug`.
- **The return.** The re-home records, for each window it moves off a monitor that left, the workspace it left and that monitor's name and description (make, model, serial), and whether the monitor showed it (`WorkspaceRemap._returns`, keyed by window id; a window re-homed twice, as a second monitor goes, keeps its first). The record is kept in the `workspaceReturns` state file with the compositor's run (`CompositorKind.sessionKey`), so a reload or restart of the shell keeps it and a new run of the compositor drops it. A second after a hotplug that brings a recorded monitor back (`_return`, also once at the shell's start), every recorded window still open goes back to the workspace it left, wherever it is now (one moved by hand too), and that workspace onto its monitor; a workspace of that id elsewhere is joined and moved over. A returning monitor showing a workspace outside its block (the compositor's pick as it connects) shows the one it showed when it left, else the lowest returned. Nothing else moves: not the focus, nor the workspace the focused monitor shows (`WorkspaceGeometry.returnWindows`, pure and tested; the backend's `returnWorkspaces`). The debug log says `returning windows to their monitor`.
  - **Which monitor is it** (`WorkspaceGeometry.returnOutput`): by description where it was known, so another monitor on the same connector (a work and a home monitor on DP-1) doesn't take the windows, which wait for their own; a monitor whose description isn't known yet (Hyprland's, a moment after it connects) isn't matched until it is. Among two alike, the one on the old connector. By connector where the description wasn't known (sway's and Hyprland's headless outputs have none, and a nested sway names a recreated output anew, so its windows wait there).
  - Records whose window has closed are dropped at each return; a layout change that renumbers the blocks (size, per-monitor or grid, a block's base) drops them all, their ids being the old layout's.
  - **On Hyprland** one Lua chunk, with the `workspaces` animation off: each window moves to its workspace, staying put (a workspace that doesn't exist is made on the window's monitor, not shown), each workspace not on its monitor moves there (`hl.dsp.workspace.move({ workspace, monitor })`, which leaves a workspace not shown unshown and the focus where it is), then a returning monitor shows its workspace (`HL.Monitor:set_workspace`, which moves no focus; checked on a nested Hyprland 0.57). **On sway** each window's monitor is focused before it moves (sway makes a workspace on the focused output), a workspace still elsewhere is shown and moved over (`move workspace to output`), and the output that had the focus has it again.
  - The standard layout (shared ids) and workspaces that belong to their output (niri, which moves them back itself) or are dynamic (Hyprland's scrolling layout) show the moved workspaces as they are, so nothing is re-homed or returned there.
  - Special workspaces (Hyprland's, ids below 1) aren't a block's: they follow the compositor.

### The grid's start
- A grid starts at its centre cell (`WorkspaceGeometry.centreWorkspace`: the middle row's middle column, after the middle with an even count), not the compositor's first id (the top left): when the grid is turned on (above), and when a run of the compositor starts in a grid.
- **A new run, not a reload.** `WorkspaceRemap` keeps the run it last started in (`CompositorKind.sessionKey`: Hyprland's instance signature, sway's socket, each new when the compositor starts) and whether the workspaces were last laid out in a grid in the `workspaces` state file (`StateManager`; `{ session, grid }`). Only a key it hasn't seen counts: a reload of the shell, or axiom restarting it, finds the same key and leaves the workspaces alone. Not in the greeter, nor with no key (and then turning the grid on doesn't centre either).
- Once the outputs and their active workspaces are known (`_ready`), `_start` sends each output still where the compositor started it (on its block's first id, or outside its block, where Hyprland numbers later monitors on) to its centre, through the backend's `applyRemap` with no moves: one batch, focused output last. An output already moved by then keeps its workspace. `goToWorkspace` isn't used: each call's slide cancels the one before (`HyprlandManager.travel`), and only the focused output's would land.
- Only where `workspaceGrid` holds (never with dynamic workspaces) and the workspaces aren't the output's own (`supports.outputWorkspaces`).

### The bar widget
- `bar/widgets/Workspaces/Workspaces.qml` loads a strip by layout:
  - standard: `workspaces/WorkspaceStrip`
  - grid (`CompositorManager.workspaceGrid`): `workspaces/WorkspaceGridStrip`, showing the active row (or column, on a vertical bar).
- **Which cells.** Without `showEmpty` (off by default; hidden with dynamic workspaces, which always leave empty ones out, the compositor's empty last one too) a strip shows the workspaces with windows and the active one; the grid strip does so within its row (`WorkspaceGridStrip.lineIds`). Down to one on a strip monitor, the strip shows alone ([workspace strips](workspace-strips.md)).
- With `wideActive`, the active `WorkspaceCell` grows by one normal cell's length (not twice its own: a long one grows by as much as a short one). The extra length is equal room on both sides of what it shows; its content stays packed together (a dot stretches into a pill).
- **What a cell shows** (`workspaces/CellContents`, for the row and both popouts; the order is `WorkspaceCells.pick`'s), highest first:
  1. the grid's active arrow (`glyphOf`)
  2. a label of one's own (`workspaceLabels`: `{ workspace, show, icon, text }` entries, `icon` an icon field's value, drawn in one row with `text` and an Icon / Text / Both switch for `show`: `x-iconText`). An entry matches the number the cell would show: its id, counted from the monitor's first with `relativeNumbers`, and where the compositor makes the workspaces itself (`CompositorManager.dynamicWorkspaces`) always its place on its monitor; one counts only where what its `show` shows isn't empty (`WorkspaceCells.labelFor`, which blanks the part it leaves out). `icon` shows the icon: a name the icon font has draws as that icon (`Utils.isSymbolName` and `IconManager.isKnown`, as the icon picker tells), anything else in the text font (`CellContents` gives it as `plain`). `text` shows the text as written (a word like `web` that is also an icon name stays a word). `both` shows the icon, by the same rule, before the text along the bar, `WorkspaceCells.iconGap` apart (`CellContents`' `lead`, `WorkspaceCell.leadIcon`); with one of them empty, the other alone
  3. app icons, on a workspace with windows (`showAppIcons`, in its `appIconsLayout`: [widget style](../bar/widget-style.md) › Workspaces cells). With `activeWorkspaceIcons: hide` (shown with app icons where strips exist and `stripWindows` isn't off) the active workspace's cell leaves them out while the strip windows after the row show (`WorkspaceCellRow.activeIcons`, from `ActiveStrip`'s `visible`), so it falls to 4 or 5; the others keep theirs
  4. with `useNames`, the compositor's name (the workspace shape's `name`) where it says more than the id or the number shown (`WorkspaceCells.nameShown`: not Hyprland's default `"3"`, nor niri's unnamed `""`), always in the text font (a workspace named `mail` isn't drawn as the mail icon)
  5. its number, dot or nothing (`labels`).
  - Only text and a Row of icons make a cell longer. Text (not an icon name) longer than a number makes its cell longer: measured with a `FontMetrics` at the cell's text size, bold, plus half a cell's thickness of room (`CellContents._length`), and with `both` the icon's length (an icon 1.2 times the bar's font size, else its text measured) and the gap. On a vertical bar such text is turned along the bar, a `both` label's icon with it (so it sits below the text, where the turned text starts), an icon of the font kept upright.
  - The strip popout's number cells show the same, less app icons (each line's windows follow it).
- **Head.** `lead` puts an icon (`leadIcon`) or the active workspace's label, as its cell shows it without app icons (`CellContents.of` on the monitor's active id: a label's icon or text, the name, else its number), before the cells in their box (`SplitLead`), in from the box's edge by the bar's padding; a split bar sets it apart as the icon part ([widget style](../bar/widget-style.md) › Split). With no box (`segmented`) it has none, so the cells' first cap ([shapes and runs](../bar/widget-shapes-runs.md)) never follows one.
- **Urgent.** A workspace a window on it marks urgent (the shape's `urgent`) draws in `urgentColor`, pulsing with `urgentPulse` ([widget style](../bar/widget-style.md) › Workspaces cells), on the bar and in both popouts.
- **Cells.** Both strips lay their cells out with `workspaces/WorkspaceCellRow`.
  - It holds every shown workspace, or one grid row.
  - Its cells are `spacing` apart (`BarsConfig.workspaceCellSpacing`: tight 0, bar the bar's `innerGap`, wide twice it), as are the popouts' cells (the payload's `cellSpacing`); the strip's window cells (`StripWindows`) take the same spacing and `solidBoxes`, but the gap between the row and the strip after it stays the bar's.
  - `glyphOf` gives the grid's position arrow.
  - Cells may differ in length. Where each sits, and the row's whole length, come from the pure `methods/WorkspaceCells` (`layout(lengths, spacing, active, extra)`, `startOf`), which the row and its `ActiveCellIndicator` both read.
  - It's measured from its cells' target lengths, never their gliding ones ([composition › Bar motion](../bar/composition.md#bar-motion-drawn-not-measured)), and clips them while one glides.
- **Active cell.** The active cell's box is one `workspaces/ActiveCellIndicator` sliding along the row.
  - It sits where the active cell starts at rest (`WorkspaceCells.startOf`: only cells after the active one move when it widens) and is as long as that cell widened, gliding to a new length as it moves.
  - It is drawn between the cells' two parts (`WorkspaceCell`'s `part`): the boxes at rest are under it, the labels and icons over it.
  - A step past an end (`CompositorManager.workspaceWrapped`, or the strip's own wheel wrap) sends it out of that end and in at the other.
  - With a bar gradient its Auto color is the gradient's where the active cell rests (`WorkspaceCellRow._activeLook`, the widget's `barSpotOf` handed down as `barSpotOf`), never where it glides ([Widget style › Bar gradient](../bar/widget-style.md#bar-gradient)).
- **Cross-fades.**
  - The grid strip cross-fades (`CrossFade`) to another row or column; the old one keeps its active cell while it fades.
  - `ActiveStrip` cross-fades to the windows of the next workspace.
- **Background.** With `showBackground` on (on a boxed style) the cells sit in the widget's box, inset by the bar's `innerGap` across (`Workspaces.inset`), so they're `thickness` less twice that across; the widget declares that as its `contentAcross`, which its box's caps keep clear of at a run's end (an arrow's point, a capsule's curve: [Shapes and runs › Backgrounds and shapes](../bar/widget-shapes-runs.md#backgrounds-and-shapes)). With `showBackground` off there's no box (`segmented`) and no inset: the cells take the bar's full `thickness` and are segments of the bar's run, each in the bar's widget shape (rounded, capsule, slant, arrow, tab), chaining on in a powerline (the row's cells then the strip's, with no divider between them there) and sitting in a merged run as widgets do, the widget keeping its place among the bar's boxed widgets (`hasBackground`) ([Shapes and runs › Parts as segments](../bar/widget-shapes-runs.md#parts-as-segments)). The popouts keep their rounded cells.
- **Scrolling.** With `scrollToSwitch` the wheel steps through the shown workspaces (the grid strip: along its row or column), or on a strip monitor scrolls the strip (`stepStrip`); `scrollReverse` turns its direction round for both.
- **Pinned start.** The widget keeps its start put while its box resizes (`pinStart`, which `BarWidgetHost` reads), so its cells don't drift as the strip after them comes and goes.
- **Options.** The strips share one schema definition. Layout-only options hide themselves with `x-showIf: { "/Workspaces.layout": … }`.
- **Grid popout.** In a grid, the widget hands its `properties` and its bar's config to the `WorkspaceGrid` popout. Its cells are the strip's own `WorkspaceCell`s, with the same:
  - size, spacing and corners
  - widget style and colors
  - labels (grid position), labels of one's own, names and app icons
  - click to switch.
- On strip monitors the widget also shows the active workspace's windows, a box each, all in one box or not at all (`stripWindows`: [Workspace strips](workspace-strips.md) › Bar).
